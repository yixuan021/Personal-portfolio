01 / THE CHALLENGE

Knowing what to say.
Feeling ready to say it.

Giving difficult feedback, disagreeing with a manager, or setting a boundary can put relationships and confidence at stake. Traditional workshops can explain communication frameworks, but leave little room for repeated, low-risk practice.

For ELEVARE Human Dynamics, our Studio NAPP team at the Centre for Digital Media (CDM) developed Project 1C: a digital training experience for practising difficult workplace conversations. The challenge was to turn a broad learning vision into a focused, testable experience.

THE DESIGN QUESTION

How might we help people practise high-stakes workplace conversations in an approachable environment, with clear guidance before, during, and after the conversation?

03 / UNDERSTANDING NEEDS

Research Insights

I contributed technical research, personas, UI breakdowns, and user flows to translate a broad training idea into a navigable experience. My strength in this work was connecting the learner's context with concrete interface decisions: what people need to understand before practice, how they move through it, and what they can review afterwards.

The team reviewed communication frameworks and existing products, identified ten workplace conversation scenarios, and explored mobile, web, individual, and organisational contexts.

04 / TESTING THE DIRECTION

Make the conversation tangible.

The team explored a physical role-play exercise before developing the digital directions. A manager–employee conversation helped reveal how different responses could affect mutual purpose, respect, and a sense of safety.

The presentation records three early lessons: branching dialogue was complex to author, scripted choices introduced unnatural pauses, and real conversations needed more nuance than fixed options allowed.

05 / THE SCOPE DECISION

A smaller experience.
A clearer next step.

The early concept combined lessons, progress tracking, AI personas, and feedback. Initial testing exposed a confusing flow: some users expected selectable responses, while the proposed prototype demanded more than the team could build within the available time.

08 / LEARNING AND ITERATING

A safe space still needs
to feel relevant.

MY USER-TESTING CONTRIBUTION

Listen to the experience around the conversation.

I conducted interviews for the AI Bot experience as part of the team's playtesting. This brought my UI/UX work into contact with how people experienced the product: whether the context made sense, how the avatar shaped expectations, and what kind of practice environment felt appropriate. The team used these findings to inform recommendations for the next iteration.

The exported product vision and art guide record different preferences across career stages. Some participants wanted a more formal, manager-like experience; others preferred a more relaxed, approachable environment.

CONTEXT

Set up the situation before the response.

Feedback on the multiple-choice experience called for clearer scenario context, more nuanced dialogue options, and a shorter interaction flow.

TONE & PRESENCE

One avatar style will not suit everyone.

AI Bot feedback informed recommendations for different avatar styles, tones, and environments. These were opportunities for further development, rather than completed customisation features.

PERSONALISATION

Keep flexibility in the longer-term direction.

The revised vision moved beyond a single formal training style toward a more adaptable practice experience, while the delivered prototypes remained focused in scope.

What the testing can—and cannot—tell us

The available materials support qualitative lessons and prototype recommendations. They do not establish production adoption, measured skill improvement, or real-world retention. Percentages shown inside the branching simulation describe fictional scenario outcomes.

09 / DELIVERY & REFLECTION

What I contributed.
What I take forward.

The team developed two complementary training prototypes. The Unity AI prototype supports free-text input, live Gemini responses, a practice avatar, and feedback after the conversation. The browser-based narrative prototype uses authored dialogue choices, with a two-player version synchronised through Firebase Firestore.

My contribution was to connect user needs, interface structure, and the experience around AI practice. I made the journey tangible through personas and flows, designed onboarding and learning-related interfaces, and brought a research perspective into AI Bot interviews. These are the strengths I bring to product design: structuring an unfamiliar experience, communicating it clearly through UI, and learning from how people use it.

From individual screens to a connected experience.

The strongest lesson I take from this project is to consider the moments before and after the core interaction. Onboarding, practice, and feedback each answer a different user question. Connecting them gives my interface work a clearer product purpose.

Make the design useful within real constraints.

Working alongside the Unity implementation reinforced the distinction between the wider learning vision and the experience the team could build and test. I take forward a stronger focus on making the immediate task clear while keeping future features separate.

Use interviews to question the first design direction.

The team heard different expectations of formality, tone, and avatar presentation. For my own practice, the lesson is to test those assumptions with users and use the findings to guide the next design question, rather than treating one visual direction as universal.
